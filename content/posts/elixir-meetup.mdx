The only Elixir I knew until last Sat was the one in Clash of clans and Clash royal. On 27th i attended bombay.ex’s first Elixir meet at Invideo office and I was thrilled to be in the presence of 50+ builders. This was one of the multiple meetups happening around the world that week weekend under the title “Global elixir meetups”

![Group Picture](/images/posts/elixir-meetup/1pic.jpeg)

A huge shoutout to Invideo Team for hosting and supporting mumbai tech meetups scene. These guys also host bombay.rs which are rust meetups.

A lot of people there were Elixir users, mostly all of them, but there were a few just curious minds too. We had an introduction round where everybody spoke about the latest interesting project they are working on. Almost 3-4 attendees mentioned the home server setup and it got me excited about that project (as I have also been planning to set up one).

So the meetup had 2 talks, first about the Introduction to Elixir by Rakshan from the Invideo team and 2nd one on how it's being used in the IDfy company by IDfy team members Jayesh and Saksham. Both the talks highlighted the importance of the Elixirs inherent distributed structure that helps in distributed systems.

Elixir is developed on top of Erlang's VM - BEAM. Erlang was developed by Ericsson in 1986. The Erlang runtime system is designed for distributed, fault-tolerant, soft real-time, highly available non-stop applications with hot code swapping.

Considering my exposure to Elixir before attending the meetup, the growth in it is exponential. Not only did I get to know about the language but also about the ecosystem around it.

After the meet up I got to meet @_svs_ and had my resume reviewed ;) He’s one of the very few people whom after meeting you'll feel like the man has some life in him and is not just cruising in the rat race. He explained the importance of blogs and thus here comes my first one.

There was an unofficial meetup for keyboard enthusiasts, Rushabh had 4 keyboards for us, 2 of them were the ergonomic (as I have seen the primeagen use them) and 2 others seemed average, but man, I was in for a surprise. That thing split in 2, it was magnetically attached, felt like my Ipad keyboard became alive. And it had its own firmware which we can configure for shortcuts and other cool stuff. Never in my life was I ever so excited to see a keyboard.

“What would you suggest for me to build as a side project in Elixir (particularly and only in Elixir) so that the Invideo Engineers would be impressed ? Some problem statement that you'd want me to solve ?” - this was the question I left Raj (Invideo team member) with before leaving the premises. He said he can't think of anything as of now, so I'll have to ask him at the next meetup again.

Fun day, Cheers.
