I had 0 expectations walking into St. Regis Mumbai where the GMAC GMAT college fair was being held. This was my first college fair. Primarily this fair was for the ones pursuing GMAT and then MBA/management degree.  

On the first stall i asked questions like a newbie, what GMAT score you require? What are the fees ? what type of profile would qualify me for this ? etc etc. but then i realised very quickly that every other person was asking the same question. I could just listen to others speak and get all the req info. 

This went on for like 3-4 stalls until the realisation started kicking in, all these colleges, their executives are here to answer your queries and all most all of the attendees were asking questions that are just one google search away. Nobody has taken a shred of effort to research their target college and ask mindful questions.

Talking of research, i also hadnt done any tbh, but i improvised and stopped asking “bachkand” questions. I moved to some insightful questions like :  
“Do you see any difference between MBA and MiM?” 
“have you seen growth in job opportunities for MiM holders?” 
“Should a graduate like me pursue MBA or should i just gather work ex, considering current markets?” 

I received a clearly better attentions span for these questions and got to hear from a lot of different perspectives of the college representatives. Through which i could figure out who is standing there just to sell their courses and who i actually there to guide students into a better choice. Trust me the later category only has 2 worthy college reps from that fair.

I also met some FCRIT co-prisoners, shreya and her elder sister. Its because of this elder sister that today i know that there is a consortium program in stanford. We had fun watching those asking stupidest question to that stanford guy. 

To shed my tiredness I went to Dadar chowpaty, very serene it was, overall a win for me.
